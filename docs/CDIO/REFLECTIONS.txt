CONCEIVE REFLECTION (Canvas)

Q1. Who are the intended users and other stakeholders, what do they need from
the product, and whose perspective might be easily overlooked? How should
this influence the project's success criteria?

  Our main users are students in a teaching lab. They need to see where
  objects are and learn what sonar can and cannot detect. Lab staff need a
  demo that is quick to set up and robust on a bench, and our team needs
  something we can build and test in 6 sessions (CONCEIVE section 2).
  The easily overlooked ones are people who aren't using it: people being
  scanned, who shouldn't be monitored without knowing, and other teams
  nearby, whose 40 kHz sensors can cross-talk with ours. Users with colour
  blindness or low vision are also easy to forget on a red/green radar
  screen. This shaped the success criteria: SC6 requires echo / no echo /
  failed to be told apart without colour, and a STALE warning within 1.5 s;
  SC10 is tested by someone outside the team with no explanation; and the
  60 ms ping gap and the T-XTALK test come from the other teams' needs.

Q2. What assumptions are you making about users' physical, sensory,
cognitive, digital or financial circumstances, or about the environment in
which the product will be used? Could any of these assumptions create a
usability or accessibility barrier?

  Sensory: we assume users can see a screen. We don't rely on colour or
  sound (every status has a shape and text), but a blind user could not
  use it. Physical: MANUAL mode assumes fine control of a joystick.
  Cognitive: we assume users can read a polar plot and know that "no echo"
  does not mean "nothing there"; SC10 tests this with an outsider. Digital:
  the display may need a PC, maybe with software installed, which a locked
  lab PC may not allow. The display is not decided yet (after S4 and B4),
  partly for this reason. Financial: kit parts only, nothing to buy.
  Environment: indoors, still air, about 20 C, objects 5 to 150 cm, but a
  noisy room with other teams' sensors (section 4). Outside that, results
  are not covered: our S2 simulation shows a board tilted more than about
  35 to 40 degrees gives no echo (docs/evidence/sim/s2_summary.txt).

Q3. What potential safety, ethical, privacy, security or sustainability issue
could arise from the proposed product or its possible use? Select the most
relevant issue and define one requirement that could address it from the
outset.

  The most relevant issue is over-trust. A radar-style screen looks
  authoritative, but ultrasound misses soft, thin or angled objects, and a
  point can be old. If someone reads "no point drawn" as "clear", that is a
  wrong conclusion in a lab and would be a collision in an
  obstacle-avoidance product (CONCEIVE section 7). Our requirement, SC6:
  the display tells echo, no echo and sensor failure apart without colour,
  visibly fades points older than one pass, and shows a STALE warning
  within 1.5 s if readings stop. It never says "clear", only "no echo".
  Tested by T-FAULT and T-STALE. We already applied this in the serial
  protocol: a missed echo is sent as "-", never as 0 cm (decision
  D-PROTO-NULL). Privacy is the second issue: no logging unless the user
  starts it, and data stays on the device or USB link (section 9).
